### 2.4.2 The formation of the opportunity pathway: direct antecedents

The opportunity pathway has its own grounding. Wrzesniewski and Dutton (2001) establish that employees are active crafters of their roles, capable of altering task and relational boundaries and the meaning of their work. This supports treating role opportunity appraisal---and its components of augmentation, elevation, and reinvention---as a genuinely agentic pathway rather than the mere absence of threat: employees may appraise generative AI as raw material for redefining what their role is and where its value lies. Recent evidence linking employees' generative AI awareness to job-crafting behavior through a sensemaking route (Yan et al., 2025) both supports this pathway and points toward the recursive process developed in the next subsection.

Grounding alone, however, does not yet answer a question of symmetry: if the opportunity pathway is a genuine competing appraisal, it requires its own formation account---conditions that generate an opportunity framing directly, not merely through the *absence* of narrative reversal or through later reappraisal in the loop. Three such direct antecedents follow from the theoretical materials already assembled, each the enabling mirror of a reversal condition.

The first is **participatory implementation**. Strategic ambiguity transfers the interpretive burden to the employee (Section 2.3.2); participation reverses the direction of that transfer. When employees are involved in deciding which tasks migrate to the system, how saved time will be used, and how their roles will be reconfigured, they become co-authors of the implementation's meaning rather than completers of an ambiguous message. Participation both supplies the specifics that constrain a threat-consistent completion and, more positively, gives employees concrete material and license for imagining augmented or elevated versions of their own role---participation is, in effect, organizationally sanctioned job crafting (Wrzesniewski & Dutton, 2001).

The second is **credible role-redesign commitment**. Word--deed consistency operates symmetrically (Section 2.3.4): just as contradictory deeds recode an enablement message as concealment, observable enabling deeds---reallocating saved time to judgment, relational, or client-facing work; funding training in AI-augmented skills; rewriting role descriptions to include new responsibilities; making verifiable redeployment rather than reduction commitments--- convert the narrative from rhetoric into evidence. Behavioral integrity (Simons, 2002) thus does not only bound the backfire mechanism; when deeds visibly enact the enablement message, they directly warrant an opportunity framing of the same implementation.

The third is **augmentation-configured demonstration**. The automation--augmentation duality (Raisch & Krakowski, 2021; developed in Section 2.5.5) implies that demonstrations are directionally open, so their configuration is itself a formative condition. Demonstrations centered on tasks employees experience as burdensome rather than identity-central, and presented together with the human judgment the output still requires, invite an elevation reading ("this absorbs what I did not value"), where an unframed demonstration of an identity-central task invites a substitution reading.

These antecedents give the opportunity pathway a formation narrative symmetrical to the threat pathway's: the pragmatic properties of Section 2.3 make backfire likely, while participation, credible role-redesign commitment, and augmentation-configured demonstration make opportunity appraisal likely. Proposition 4a states this claim directly. Appraisal theory thus performs two functions for the framework: it legitimizes the dual pathway, in which threat and opportunity appraisals arise from one input and are adjudicated by secondary appraisal; and it converts the outcome from a quantity of adoption into a mode of response. What it does not yet explain is how appraisals are *revised* over time---which requires characterizing how the technology's own outputs feed the appraisal process.
