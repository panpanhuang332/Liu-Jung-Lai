### 2.3.5 Automation anxiety and AI threat as the threat-laden backdrop

The employee who receives an enablement narrative is not a neutral interpreter, and the literatures on job insecurity and AI-related threat specify the priors that make reversal likely. Job insecurity research establishes that perceived insecurity---the anticipated, subjective threat of job loss---exerts powerful effects on attitudes and well-being, and is driven more by anticipation than by realized events (De Witte, 1999; Sverke et al., 2002; Shoss, 2017). Research on STARA (smart technology, artificial intelligence, robotics, and algorithms) awareness shows that employees are conscious of, and anxious about, the prospect of such technologies displacing them (Brougham & Haar, 2018). Research on AI identity threat shows that AI can threaten occupational *identity*, not only employment, unsettling the sense of distinctiveness and value attached to a professional role (Mirbabaie et al., 2022). These literatures supply the interpretive default many employees bring to implementation: a prior that already leans toward substitution. An enablement message that is pragmatically marked, strategically ambiguous, and euphemistically worded thus meets a receiver primed to complete it in threat-consistent terms.
