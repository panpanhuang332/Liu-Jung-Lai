# 6. Conclusion

This paper began from a simple observation: employees may resist generative AI before they meaningfully use it, because they first ask not whether the technology is useful but what its implementation means for their role. Building on that observation, the paper has relocated the explanation of divergent employee responses from the evaluation of technological attributes to the formation of role meaning, and has specified two coupled mechanisms through which that meaning forms.

What distinguishes the account is neither mechanism alone but their coupling. A narrative can be pragmatically turned against its intent; the employee then adjudicates that reading against evidence of their own making, probing a system that answers back. The frame selects the probes, the probes return the cues, and the cues---filtered by trust, coping resources, and credibility---decide whether the frame entrenches, oscillates, or reverses. Because every step is tied to observable message properties, appraisal components, and use trajectories, the framework stakes claims that an empirical program can overturn. The most pointed of these is practical as well as theoretical: usage volume alone cannot distinguish an empowered workforce from a frightened one.

The framework's central claim is therefore that divergent and unstable responses to the same system can be explained not only by differences in the technology but also by how employees construct, and recursively revise, its meaning for their roles. Understood this way, generative AI implementation is not only a problem of technology adoption but a recursive process of employee role meaning formation---one whose propositions offer a basis for future empirical testing.
