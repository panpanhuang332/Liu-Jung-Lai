### 2.5.5 Demonstrative cues, task-role overlap, and the automation--augmentation duality

**Demonstrative cues**---the system performing a task---convert a hypothetical possibility into an observed event and are, for that reason, typically more consequential than self-description. Their impact on appraisal depends on *task-role overlap*: the more central the demonstrated task is to the employee's role identity, the stronger the appraisal it provokes, in either direction. The direction is not fixed by the demonstration itself. Raisch and Krakowski's (2021) automation--augmentation paradox captures the underlying duality: the same capability can be read as automating (substituting for) or augmenting (extending) human work, and the two are entangled rather than opposed. An employee who watches generative AI perform an identity-central task may extract substitution ("this is what I do, done without me") or elevation ("this frees me for higher-order work"), and which reading dominates is governed by secondary appraisal resources and cue credibility rather than by the demonstration alone. Because performance is uneven, moreover, self-run demonstrations also return *disconfirming* evidence, so appraisals may be revised rather than only amplified---a central source of the instability the framework predicts.
