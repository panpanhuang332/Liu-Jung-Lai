## 1.2 Two coupled mechanisms: an overview

The paper theorizes two coupled mechanisms that govern how that meaning forms and what follows from it. This section introduces the mechanisms and the constructs they require only far enough to orient the reader; full definitions and theoretical grounding are deferred to Sections 2 and 3, and are not repeated here.

The first mechanism, **enablement narrative backfire**, explains how communication intended to frame generative AI as empowering, supportive, or developmental can instead be interpreted as evidence of role substitution, professional devaluation, or future displacement. The reversal is not merely contextual---a matter of low trust---but is pragmatically produced by identifiable properties of the message itself: marked reassurance, strategic ambiguity, euphemistic efficiency vocabulary, and word--deed inconsistency, developed in Section 2.3. Its output is the **AI-as-substitute frame**: an employee-level interpretive frame through which implementation is read as evidence that AI may replace, absorb, displace, or downgrade meaningful parts of one's professional role. Section 2.3.6 distinguishes this frame from a general negative attitude toward AI, and Section 3.2 from the appraisals it feeds.

The frame connects to responses through a dual pathway of appraisal. **Role threat appraisal** is the self-referential appraisal that valued components of one's own role---employment security, the market value of expertise, role autonomy, and occupational identity and status---are endangered. **Role opportunity appraisal** is the appraisal that those same components can be augmented, elevated, or reinvented. Section 2.4 grounds both pathways in cognitive appraisal theory, specifies the direct antecedents of the opportunity pathway, and explains why the threat pathway is the focal risk this paper theorizes without being the only pathway; Section 3.3 specifies the components of each appraisal.

The second mechanism, the **AI-interactive role sensemaking loop**, explains why frame formation is only the beginning of the process. Unlike earlier workplace technologies, whose meaning employees inferred indirectly by observing organizational consequences over time, generative AI supplies employees with a direct, immediate, and largely private means of generating their own role-relevant evidence: it can be queried in natural language, prompted to demonstrate tasks that overlap with one's role, elicited for statements about its own capabilities, and tested outside managerial framing and control. Employees therefore probe the system, extract cues from what it says and does, and recursively update their appraisals---entrenching, revising, or reversing the initial frame. Section 2.5 develops the loop's theoretical foundations and situates it within its social context.

Appraisals issue in one of four **modes of adoption response**---avoidance, compliance-only use, defensive adoption, and genuine integration---distinguished by driver, discretion, and behavioral depth, and formalized in Section 3.4. The taxonomy's pivotal member is defensive adoption: self-initiated, intensified use driven by role threat and aimed at remaining indispensable, often high in volume yet shallow in integration. The mode is not hypothetical: multinational survey evidence indicates that substantial shares of employees use generative AI covertly and present its output as their own, with fear of appearing replaceable among the reported motives (Gillespie et al., 2025). Role threat can thus produce avoidance, compliance-only use, or defensive adoption, depending on the employee's secondary appraisal of coping resources; genuine integration requires the opportunity pathway or a successful reappraisal reached through the loop.

It is the *coupling* of the two mechanisms---an initial frame that channels which probes an employee runs, and probes that return evidence capable of entrenching or overturning that frame---that mainstream technology adoption models do not specify, and that explains why the same system produces divergent and unstable role appraisals across a workforce.
