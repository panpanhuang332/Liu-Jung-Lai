### 2.5.7 Emerging generative AI sensemaking research and the remaining gap

Recent work has begun to bring a sensemaking lens to generative AI. Agarwal et al. (2026) analyze how users make sense of, and give sense about, generative AI "teammates" through user reviews, showing that meaning-making around the technology is active and social. Yan et al. (2025) show that employees' generative AI awareness shapes job-crafting behavior through a sensemaking process that can move toward approach or avoidance. These studies establish that sensemaking is the appropriate lens for generative AI at work and that divergence between approach and avoidance responses is real. Parallel dual-pathway findings are accumulating in the job-crafting literature (e.g., Xu et al., 2026), and the present taxonomy should be read against them: crafting orientations describe how employees reshape a role they retain, whereas modes of adoption response classify the stance taken toward the technology itself---including compliance-only use and defensive adoption, which crafting categories do not distinguish. What remains less fully specified---and what the present paper supplies---is a theorization of these outputs as a *recursive source of role-relevant cues* that employees enact, credit, and appraise against specific components of their role, together with a connection between that cue-production process and the reversal of an organizational enablement narrative. The gap, in short, is one of mechanism specification, not the absence of a sensemaking perspective.
