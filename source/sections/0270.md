### 2.5.4 Cue credibility: the MAIN model and the machine heuristic

Whether a cue shifts an appraisal depends on whether it is *credited*. Sundar's (2008) MAIN model identifies interface cues---modality, agency, interactivity, and navigability---that trigger cognitive heuristics about credibility. Among these, the *machine heuristic*---the belief that machines are objective, unbiased, mechanical, and therefore accurate---can lead people to trust machine-sourced information more than human-sourced information in certain contexts (Sundar & Kim, 2019; Yang & Sundar, 2024). Applied here, the machine heuristic implies that employees who regard generative AI as objective may over-credit its self-referential and demonstrative cues as valid evidence about their own replaceability, strengthening a threat appraisal. Conversely, awareness of the technology's unevenness---its propensity to hallucinate, its brittleness, its shallow grasp of specialized domains---supplies grounds for discounting cues, weakening the appraisal shift or prompting revision. This is the theoretical basis for treating **AI-generated cue credibility** as a boundary condition: the same demonstration moves an appraisal only to the extent that the employee treats it as trustworthy evidence about the role.
