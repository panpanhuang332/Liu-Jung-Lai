## 1.4 Research gap, research questions, and contributions

The gap addressed here is not that prior research has ignored employee interpretation, nor that AI-related threat has never been studied; both are well established. The gap is a missing **mechanism specification**. Existing work can name the ingredients of the present problem---frame incongruence, sensegiving, job insecurity, identity threat, algorithmic awareness---but it does not specify, first, how an explicitly positive managerial narrative is pragmatically reversed into a substitution signal, and second, how generative AI's own outputs become role-relevant cues in the interpretation of implementation. This paper specifies the first as enablement narrative backfire operating through the AI-as-substitute frame, and the second as the AI-interactive role sensemaking loop. Accordingly, the paper addresses three research questions:

**RQ1:** Through what pragmatic mechanisms do organizational enablement narratives become reinterpreted as substitution signals during generative AI implementation?

**RQ2:** How does the AI-as-substitute frame shape role threat and role opportunity appraisals, and how do these appraisals translate into distinct *modes* of adoption response rather than a single level of adoption intention?

**RQ3:** How do employee--AI interactions recursively update role appraisals and adoption responses, and under what conditions---of organizational trust, secondary appraisal resources, and cue credibility---do they entrench rather than revise an initial substitution frame?

In answering these questions, the paper makes three contributions, all resting on a single relocation: it moves the explanation of divergent employee responses upstream, from the evaluation of technological attributes to the formation of role meaning. First, it specifies **a mechanism of narrative reversal**, advancing frame and sensemaking research from *documenting incongruence* to *specifying reversal*: how a positive managerial frame is transformed into an employee substitution frame through identifiable pragmatic properties. Second, it develops **a recursive cue-production account of generative AI sensemaking**, explaining why direct interaction with the *same* system can intensify threat for one employee and open opportunity for another, and why role appraisals remain unstable rather than settling: the technology does not only communicate about work---it performs work-like outputs that employees appraise against their own role. Third, it offers **a response-mode view of adoption under role appraisal**, reframing the outcome of AI implementation from a quantity (how much adoption) to a kind (what kind of adoption), and specifying why role threat need not, and often does not, simply depress use. The paper's claim to novelty rests on these three elements alone---narrative reversal, recursive cue production, and the response-mode taxonomy---and the remaining theoretical apparatus plays a supporting role in their service. These are theoretical contributions with a clear practical corollary---generative AI implementation is as much a matter of meaning management as of technical readiness or user training---but the paper develops the mechanisms rather than reducing them to communication advice.
