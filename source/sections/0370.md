## 3.4 A taxonomy of adoption response modes

The paper's third contribution is formalized here. Rather than a single point on an adoption-intention scale, the outcome is a set of qualitatively distinct modes distinguished by their *driver*, their *behavioral depth*, and the *role meaning* they express. Table 3 presents the taxonomy; three distinctions do the analytic work. First, avoidance and compliance-only use are both low in engagement, but avoidance is self-chosen refusal where the employee can decline, whereas compliance-only use is externally mandated and undertaken only to satisfy a requirement; in Figure 1, the italic label *(external mandate)* attached to the compliance-only-use mode marks precisely this condition, under which role threat issues in compliance-only use rather than in avoidance or defensive adoption. Second, compliance-only use and defensive adoption can look alike behaviorally once engagement is mandated, but compliance carries no protective motive while defensive adoption is threat-driven and self-protective. Third, defensive adoption and genuine integration can resemble each other in volume yet differ in driver (threat vs. opportunity) and in depth (shallow and unstable vs. deep and stable); genuine integration is the one mode not reachable from threat alone, arising instead from a role opportunity appraisal or a successful reappraisal achieved through the loop. Because the drivers are motivational states, the taxonomy is rendered falsifiable through the behavioral signatures each driver implies---discretionary excess versus mandated minimum, depth versus volume, and threat-contingent versus opportunity-anchored stability. Modes are therefore classified by observable use trajectories, while motives remain the explanatory mechanism rather than the classification criterion; Section 4 states the mode propositions in these observable terms.
