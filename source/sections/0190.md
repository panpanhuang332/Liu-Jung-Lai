### 2.3.6 Synthesis: a pragmatic reversal mechanism, not frame incongruence

Read together, these five literatures do more than enumerate reasons for anxiety; they specify a *pragmatic reversal mechanism*. Meaning is co-produced in context, and to the extent that reassurance is pragmatically marked (2.3.1), consequential specifics are strategically withheld (2.3.2), efficiency vocabulary carries economic connotations (2.3.3), managerial deeds contradict words against a low-trust and previously breached background (2.3.4), and the receiver is primed toward substitution (2.3.5), an explicitly positive enablement narrative becomes increasingly likely to be reversed into an AI-as-substitute frame. These conditions operate cumulatively rather than as jointly necessary requirements: each raises the likelihood of reversal, and their co-occurrence makes it substantially more probable. This is what distinguishes the present claim from the established finding of frame incongruence. Incongruence is two parties reading the same technology differently; reversal is a single message being turned into evidence for the opposite of its intent. The mechanism assembled here is what Section 1.2 named enablement narrative backfire, and its output---the AI-as-substitute frame---is the interpretive input to the appraisal process developed next.
