## 4.1 From enablement narrative to the AI-as-substitute frame

Section 2.3 specified narrative reversal as a cumulative rather than conjunctive process. The propositions separate the properties of the message itself (P1, with its converse P1a) from the standing contextual conditions that amplify them (P2) and from the trust boundary condition that moderates the link (P3), so that the three tiers of Section 3 appear as three distinct propositional forms.

**P1.** *The likelihood that an organizational enablement narrative is reversed into an AI-as-substitute frame increases with the degree to which the message is (i) pragmatically marked by reassurance, (ii) strategically ambiguous about which tasks and how many roles will remain, and (iii) euphemistically framed in efficiency vocabulary; each property independently increases the likelihood of a threat-consistent completion, and the co-occurrence of the three makes reversal more probable than any single property alone.*

**P1a.** *Conversely, the likelihood of reversal decreases with message specificity: enablement narratives that specify task scope, role consequences, and observable organizational commitments constrain threat-consistent completion, so that specificity operates as the message-level counterweight to the reversal properties stated in P1.*

**P2.** *These wording properties operate against contextual antecedents that raise the baseline probability of reversal: perceived role vulnerability, low participation in implementation decisions, and word--deed inconsistency between managerial rhetoric and observable organizational action each increase the likelihood that an enablement narrative is read through a substitution-oriented frame, and their co-occurrence further amplifies this likelihood.*

**P3.** *Organizational trust moderates the narrative-to-frame link: the reversal specified in P1 and amplified by P2 is stronger when trust---perceived ability, benevolence, and integrity---is low and is attenuated when trust is high, so that the same pragmatically marked message is more readily taken at face value under high trust.*
