### 2.5.1 Enacted sensemaking: probing produces the cues

Weick's (1988) account of enacted sensemaking holds that actors produce part of the environment they subsequently confront and interpret, so that action and interpretation are recursively coupled; in crisis, acting generates the very cues that render the situation sensible---or more dangerous. Transposed to generative AI, this describes the employee who asks the system what it can do, or sets it a task drawn from their own workflow: the probe is an action that produces the very cue the employee then interprets. This is why sensemaking about generative AI is not a one-time reception of a managerial message but an ongoing, enacted process. The employee is simultaneously the author of the probe and the interpreter of its result, and each interpretation conditions the next probe---the structural feature that turns interpretation into a loop rather than a single reception.
