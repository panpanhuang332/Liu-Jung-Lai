## Public Interest Statement

Many organizations introduce generative AI with reassuring messages: the technology will assist employees, not replace them. Yet employees often hear the opposite. This paper explains why well-intended "enablement" messages can backfire---reassurance itself raises the possibility of replacement, vague promises leave employees to fill in the gaps, and efficiency language can sound like code for job cuts. It also explains why responses to the same AI system differ so widely: employees can now test the technology themselves, privately asking it to perform their own tasks, and what they see feeds back into how threatened or empowered they feel. The result is not simply more or less adoption but different kinds of adoption---avoiding the tool, using it only as required, using it intensively out of fear, or genuinely integrating it into one's role. Understanding these dynamics can help organizations communicate more honestly and implement AI more humanely.
