## 3.1 Architecture of the framework

The framework couples the two mechanisms in sequence and closes them into a loop. An organizational enablement narrative is received by an employee whose interpretation is pre-loaded by contextual antecedents. Through *enablement narrative backfire*, and moderated by organizational trust, that narrative can be reversed into an *AI-as-substitute frame*. The frame biases primary appraisal toward threat; secondary appraisal resources then shape whether it stabilizes as *role threat appraisal* or *role opportunity appraisal*. The *AI-interactive role sensemaking loop* runs recursively on that appraisal: the employee probes generative AI, which produces self-referential and demonstrative cues; moderated by AI-generated cue credibility, these cues update the appraisal, and the updated appraisal conditions the next probe. Finally, the appraisal process, under varying levels of discretion and external mandate, channels the employee into one of four *modes of adoption response*. Two features distinguish this architecture from a linear adoption model: the appraisal is revisable rather than fixed, because the loop feeds fresh evidence back into it; and the terminal outcome is a *kind* of response, not a *level* of use. Figure 1 depicts the full framework.

One clarification bounds the loop. Its distinctive cue source is the artifact's own outputs, because that is the channel prior accounts leave unspecified; it is not the only channel through which appraisals move. Two further channels are recognized. First, socially transmitted cues---colleagues' demonstrations, circulating AI-generated outputs, a manager's observed use---enter the loop directly as a third cue class (Section 2.5.6), subject to source-credibility rather than machine-heuristic moderation. Second, organizational actions that occur during implementation---restructuring announcements, hiring decisions, revised targets---also update the appraisal, but they enter by revising the contextual reading, most directly perceived word--deed consistency, rather than through the cue channels of the loop. Word--deed consistency is accordingly an antecedent at the moment the narrative is received and a continuing stream of organizational evidence thereafter; the framework foregrounds the artifact channel without claiming exclusivity for it.

The framework distinguishes three tiers. **Primary mechanisms and constructs** carry the two mechanisms and lie on the main path from narrative to response. **Contextual antecedents** precede and shape the interpretive process before any appraisal forms. **Boundary conditions** moderate specific links within it. Keeping the tiers distinct is not cosmetic: an element's tier determines where it enters the model and, in Section 4, the form of the proposition in which it appears---antecedents raise or lower a baseline probability, boundary conditions strengthen or weaken a specified link, and primary mechanisms and constructs occupy the causal path itself.
